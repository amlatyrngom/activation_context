# POC training-friendliness review — the deep-intervention path after its first GPU run (NDI vs NDC)

Reviewed 2026-09-16 from the mirrors `IB/TMP/SYNC/AC_RECON_ORCD/{NDI,NDC}/` (`report_data.json`, `training_stats.json`, `result.json`, `console.log`) and the working tree (`activation/ac_model/ac_model.py`, `ac_model_utils.py`, `ac_model_training.py`, `ac_model_reporter.py`, `activation/harness/hf_utils.py`). Read-only; nothing was run on a node. Numbers are from the traces; "rel." means a delta scale divided by that layer's calibrated residual RMS; "Adam bound" means the largest move Adam can give a scalar over an interval (the sum of its learning rate over that interval).

## Verdict

**REVISE the recipe before more deep arms run; the mechanism is neither confirmed nor refuted.** The run's one robust readout is real and growing (`held − deltas_off`: 0.007 → 0.054 nats over the 8 panels, 97 % of the 300 panel items improved), but the traces are dominated by an optimiser artefact: every delta scale walked upward at 40–80 % of the Adam bound for the whole run and all five ended at the same absolute value (0.026–0.031 above their start) because a scalar in an Adam group moves by about its learning rate per update, whatever the layer. The rel. scales therefore ended ordered by 1/RMS (22 % at layer 7 down to 3.6 % at layer 31), the deep layers were still asking for more when the cosine decay stopped the walk, and the deltas' measured worth tracked the magnitude they were allowed rather than anything the heads learned. So the run cannot separate "under-trained magnitude" from "information-starved heads" (`DEEP_ARCHITECTURES.md`), and NDI's faster learning of the shared weights cannot be separated from onset chaos (NDC 0.981 vs ND 0.712 on the same recipe). Objective coverage is correct: every forward that should carry the payload does, none that should not does, and the `deltas_off` panel is the same model minus its payload. Two code changes are needed before the next arms (scale parametrisation in units of the calibrated RMS with its own group; an explicit input-only → deep checkpoint migration so warm-started deep arms can run), plus four cheap controls that would settle the information question.

## Findings

### 1. Scale dynamics — an Adam-bound walk, not a learned magnitude (HIGH)

- **All layers moved by the same absolute amount.** Scale drift over 1,000 updates: layer 7 +0.028, 11 +0.026, 19 +0.031, 23 +0.031, 31 +0.026, against an Adam bound of 0.056 (Σ lr of the AC group, warm-up 50 + cosine 1e-4 → 1e-5). Final rel. scales 0.220 / 0.168 / 0.131 / 0.078 / 0.035 — the ratio 6.3 between layer 7 and 31 is the RMS ratio 7.6 (0.132 vs 1.009), not a preference. The task brief's "1 % → 9 % in 200 updates at layer 7, then settled" is only half right: layer 7 was at 10.5 % at update 200 and kept walking to 22 %.
- **The walk was gradient-limited nowhere until late, and never at the deep layers.** Step / Adam bound (≈ the sign-consistency of "grow" seen by Adam): updates 0–100: 0.75 / 0.70 / 0.78 / 0.77 / 0.82; 100–500: 0.49 / 0.45 / 0.49 / 0.43 / 0.38; 800–1,000: **0.37 / 0.33 / 0.54 / 0.77 / 0.53**. Layers 7 and 11 half-saturated near 0.22 / 0.17; layers 19, 23, 31 were still LR-limited at the end (layer 23 at 0.77 of the bound). Compare the input row's own gate `target_head.scale`, which plateaued: 0.63 of the bound in the first 100 updates, 0.22 in the last 200, ending at 0.0328 = 2.6× the embedding RMS (NDC: 0.60 → 0.23, 0.0289). The deep gates are under-trained; the row gate is not.
- **The deltas' worth tracked the allowed magnitude.** `held − deltas_off` per panel: 0.000, 0.007, 0.018, 0.024, 0.032, 0.042, 0.047, 0.055, 0.054 (novel 0.046, wiki 0.072 at the end) while the mean rel. scale went 0.010, 0.041, 0.072, 0.088, 0.102, 0.113, 0.121, 0.126, 0.128. The gap flattened only in the last eighth, when the LR had decayed to ~1.5e-5 — the schedule stopped it, not the gradient.
- **Effective learning rate of the magnitude depends on the layer.** `DeltaHead.forward` is `unit_rows(ffn(z)) * scale` (`ac_model_utils.py:508-519`), `scale` an absolute scalar calibrated to `0.01 × rms_l` (`ac_model.py:75`, `:336-340`) and placed in the AC group at 1e-4 (`ac_model_training.py:509-524`; NDI has no gate group). A step of lr in absolute units is lr/rms_l in relative units: 7.6× larger at layer 7 than at layer 31. The reporter widget's own caption says the trajectory is "expected flat at the encoder rate" (`ac_model_reporter.py:146`) — i.e. this was foreseen but the consequence (layer-dependent relative step, deep layers starved of magnitude) was not.
- **The 1 % init is too small under this walk.** Reaching 20 % of RMS at layer 31 needs 0.2 absolute ≈ 2,000 updates at the maximum step; the run had 1,024 with decay. A second reason: the payload is cast to the reader's bf16 residual at the add (`hf_utils.py:173`, `rows[keep].to(dtype=hidden.dtype)`, reader dtype `model_config.py:55`). A delta at 1 % of the residual is 2^-6.6 of the summand; bf16 keeps 2^-8 relative precision, so at init each delta element survives with 2–3 quantisation levels (typical elements; outlier channels worse). The backward through the add is exact, so learning proceeds, but the forward effect of the deltas at init is coarse. At 5–10 % the same arithmetic gives 13–26 levels.

### 2. Gradient balance — Adam makes the heads neither starved nor dominant; the informative gradient is not logged (MEDIUM)

- Head grad norms (before clipping, `ac_model_training.py:635-636`, `:1208-1210`) in the last 200 updates: 0.23 / 0.23 / 0.21 / 0.16 / 0.03 vs encoder 8–12 and reader 2.2–2.5. Joint clipping at 1.0 (`clip_per_group=False`, `:637-643`) was binding at every step (joint norm 5–27), so the heads' raw gradients were scaled by 1/8–1/27 — but so was everything else, and Adam's per-parameter normalisation removes a common factor except as a per-step reweighting. No starvation, no dominance.
- **Head grad norms are proportional to the scale, by construction.** Because the output is `unit_rows(...) * scale`, the gradient of the head weights scales with `scale`; the last-200-update ratio grad / rel. scale is 1.04 / 1.35 / 1.57 / 2.06 / 0.87 at the five layers. The rising `intervention_grads` widget therefore reports the scale walk, not learning; and the collapse that would signal information starvation cannot appear while the scale grows. The gradient that matters — what each head sends back into the shared summary rows `rows` (`ac_model.py:522-523`, the same tensor `target_head` reads) — is not recorded.
- **Faster learning of the shared weights: there is a mechanism, but this pair cannot prove it.** NDI's `deltas_off` panel (rows only, same shared weights) was 1.399 at 0.25 vs NDC 1.741, and NDC sat in a plateau from 0.125 to 0.375 (1.705 → 1.741 → 1.644; encoder grad norm 3–6 in updates 192–384) that NDI never entered (encoder norm 20–27 in updates 64–576). Mechanism in the code: five extra gradient paths into `rows`, each entering the reader at a full-attention block input (layers 7/11 read the row's K/V directly) and bypassing the seven GDN blocks and the input row's own walk to audibility — the encoder hears "make content" earlier. But the ledger's finding 5 (R0F vs R0F3, same recipe, transition at 3k updates vs never) and ND 0.712 vs NDC 0.981 say onset is chaotic at this size, so the 0.18-nat rows-only lead is inside the noise. `NDIg` does not isolate this (its heads still feed `rows`); recommendation 3 gives the control that does.

### 3. Objective coverage — correct; `deltas_off` is what it claims; two product-side guards missing (LOW for the bench, MEDIUM before the agent path)

- Payload present: batched student loss (`_batch_loss`, `ac_model_training.py:1121` `encode_batch(with_layers=True)`, `:1129` `_assemble_student`, `:1131` → `_padded_forward` `:1175-1176`), unbatched student loss (`_example_loss` `:1042-1050`), every reporting panel (`_evaluate` `:986`, `:993` pass `deltas_off` through; right padding keeps positions physical), greedy samples at prefill only (`:873-884` → `generate_with_interventions`, `hf_utils.py:201-230`; decode steps see offset ≥ prompt and add nothing).
- Payload absent, correctly: the no-context penalty reference (`:1156`, rows and deltas both stripped with the parts), the live base pass (`:1159`), the stored references, calibration (`ac_model.py:329-333`). There is no forward where deltas are present and the loss blind to them, nor one where they are missing and should count.
- `deltas_off` (`:1191-1192`): rows kept, payload `None`, parameters untouched (scales are parameters, never modified), so it is the trained model minus its payload. Two caveats for reading it: the heads are still computed (`encode_batch(with_layers=True)`; wasted compute only), and it measures the inference-time contribution only — the training-time effect on the shared weights (finding 2) is visible only against NDC.
- Missing guards: (a) `encode()` / `encode_batch(with_layers=False)` (`ac_model.py:397-398`) silently returns rows only, so any consumer other than this trainer (agent rollouts, the queue) would drop a deep model's deltas without a trace; (b) `load()` refuses an input-only checkpoint into a deep model (`ac_model.py:581-583`), which is right for accidental mixes but blocks the warm-started deep arm (`init_ac` from K16r1 with `interventions 4`, recommendation 4) until an explicit migration exists.

### 4. Information source — the traces cannot show starvation, and the little they say leans toward re-presentation (MEDIUM)

- The two signatures the brief names are both masked: head grads do not collapse because they are tied to the scale (finding 2), and the rel. scales do not stall at a common fraction because their spread is 1/rms_l (finding 1).
- What the run does say: (i) the gain is broad and shallow — per item, `deltas_off − held` has median 0.050, mean 0.054, max 0.214, min −0.038, positive on 290/300 items. New information (a digit, a name) would give a heavy tail on the items that need it; a uniform shift is what a per-layer re-presentation of the same `z` gives. (ii) The reader still asked for louder deltas at 22 % of the residual RMS at layer 7 while the input row already sits at 2.6× the embedding RMS: "make the row's content louder at every depth" is what one would expect if the deltas carry the same content as the row. Both are weak; recommendations 2 and 3 give the cheap decisive controls.

### 5. Early-training confounds — none visible (LOW)

- Penalty traces are identical within noise (window means NDI 0.017 → 0.009, NDC 0.017 → 0.010; final 0.0111 vs 0.0112); no-context floor 1.869 vs 1.866; the penalty's reference forward carries neither rows nor deltas, so it cannot fight them. Reader grad norm ended 2.5 vs 2.2 (+14 %), no sign of the LoRA working against the deltas, and `held − deltas_off ≤ 0.055` says the reader never became dependent on them. The one latent confound is that the growing perturbation is a non-stationary input for the reader LoRA; the cosine decay hid it here, a constant-LR run would not.
- Calibration is the mean over all positions of eight no-row sequences (`hf_utils.py:182-196`, `ac_model.py:335`): the first token's outsized residual is in that mean, and the deltas are added at row positions only, where the residual is the row's own processed state. The honest denominator (residual RMS at the row positions of the student sequence, reader as trained) is not measured.

### 6. Parametrisation constraint worth an arm (LOW)

`unit_rows` (`ac_model_utils.py:430-433`) forces every delta row at every layer to exactly `scale` RMS; a head cannot be quiet for a row or a layer where it has nothing to add. For the input row that is harmless (the reader must read something); for a residual perturbation it means every row carries a fixed-magnitude vector, useful or not.

## Recommendations (priority order, each with its readout)

1. **Reparametrise the scale and give it a group — before any further deep arm.** `scale_l = rms_l × s_l` with `s_l` the trained parameter (init 0.05, not 0.01: bf16 resolution and walk length, finding 1), in the `delta_scales` gate group (`ac_model_training.py:517-519`) at 1e-3–3e-3. As pre-registered, `NDIg` moves an *absolute* scalar at 3e-3, i.e. 2.3 % of RMS per update at layer 7 and 0.3 % at layer 31 — it inherits the 7.6× disparity; fix the units first so that `NDIg` means the same thing at every layer. Readout: step / Adam bound falls below ~0.2 at every layer well before the decay (a real plateau), and `held − deltas_off` saturates; the plateau gap is the mechanism's ceiling at this size. If it stays ≤ 0.06, deeper injection of `z` is not worth more bytes.
2. **Frozen-random-heads control (`NDI-R`).** Same as 1 but `up`/`down` frozen at init, only `s_l` trained. Readout: if `held − deltas_off` reaches most of NDI's (≥ 0.03), the learned content is irrelevant and the deltas act as a per-layer amplifier of the row — go straight to richer sources (A1 layer-matched readout, A2, B1 in `DEEP_ARCHITECTURES.md`) and stop tuning these heads. If it stays near 0, the heads do learn something and 1 measures how much.
3. **Detached-source control (`NDI-D`).** `delta_heads[l](rows.detach())` at `ac_model.py:522`: the heads learn, the encoder does not hear them. Run as a pair with NDC (and a second seed pair, given the 0.27-nat spread). Readout: if NDI-D's rows-only (`deltas_off`) curve falls back onto NDC's while NDI's stays ahead, the five gradient paths into `rows` are a training aid on their own — worth keeping even if the inference-time payload is dropped (train deep, serve shallow).
4. **Warm-started deep arm (`NDI-W`).** Encoder + reader from a converged 1/16 checkpoint (K16r1 epoch 2, 0.210; the bench's `init_ac` / `init_reader`), fresh heads calibrated on the warm reader, 1 above. Needs the explicit migration in `load()` (`ac_model.py:581-583`): load the shared modules, build heads, calibrate, mark the checkpoint deep. Readout: `held − deltas_off` at a converged encoder, against the WCa/WCb warm-start noise floor — no onset chaos, no shared-weight confound; a gap above the WCa–WCb spread is the cleanest number this POC can produce.
5. **Log what the question needs.** (a) Per layer, the gradient norm into `rows` through each head vs through `target_head` (five `torch.autograd.grad` calls on one micro-batch every 25 updates, or hooks on `rows`), so finding 2 is measured rather than argued; (b) residual RMS at the row positions per layer at each panel (reader as trained), and the scale divided by *that*; (c) cosine of each delta with the residual at its position and with `target_head(z)` (does the delta re-inject the row?); (d) attention mass from target tokens onto rows at layers 7/11 with and without the payload on 8 panel items (eager attention); (e) the per-item `deltas_off − held` distribution in `result.json`.
6. **Smaller code items before more deep arms.** Guard `encode_batch(with_layers=False)` for deep models (raise unless explicitly allowed); use the median over positions (or drop the first token) in `capture_layer_input_rms`; keep the head norms but caption the widget "proportional to the scale"; and test the per-row gate as an arm (`scale × sigmoid(w·z + b)`, `b` set so the gate starts at the calibrated fraction) once 1–2 have run.

## Closing

Verdict: **REVISE** — the deep path is correctly wired and the deltas are worth 0.046 novel / 0.072 wiki nats at inference, but the scale parametrisation turned the run into an Adam-bound walk that leaves the deep layers under-trained and the information question open. Order of work: (1) rms-unit scale parametrisation + gate group, (2) frozen-random-heads control, (3) detached-source control, (4) warm-started deep arm with the checkpoint migration, (5) the five extra logs, (6) the small guards.

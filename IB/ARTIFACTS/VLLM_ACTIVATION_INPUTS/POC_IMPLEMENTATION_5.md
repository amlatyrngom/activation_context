## The problem this addresses

Every deep variant so far (residual deltas from the summary, the layer-matched side state or passage attention; additive K/V deltas; scaled or lora-style heads; row-position calibration; heads-only staging) lands on the input-only control: 0.12 at 1/16 and, decisively, at 1/32 where the rows are clearly short (A32DnF 0.323 vs the input-only A32X 0.322, deltas off 0.333). All of those designs add information at the **row positions** (the store side): every target token still has to fetch it through its own attention onto 1/16 or 1/32 of the positions, so the fetch side stays the bottleneck, and the residual errors are verbatim identifiers and numbers — a lookup problem. Round 5 builds the two designs that change what the target tokens **read** rather than what the rows carry, plus the instrument that bounds the whole family.

## A. `intervention_target = "kv_slots"` — the rows' K/V become side-computed slots

At each chosen full-attention reader layer (the same resolution as before: 4 interior full-attention blocks plus the last layer on Qwen3.5-4B → 7/11/19/23/31), the pre-RoPE keys and values of the **row positions** are **replaced** by

    K[rows] = keep_k · K_reader[rows] + slot_k(side state),   keep_k = 1 − takeover_k
    V[rows] = keep_v · V_reader[rows] + slot_v(side state),   keep_v = 1 − takeover_v

where `slot_k`, `slot_v` are the two halves of a per-layer bottleneck head `KVSlotHead` (`d_side → r → 2·d_kv`, SiLU, `r = 640`; `activation/ac_model/ac_model_utils.py:654-692`) applied to the side model's state at the row positions, and `takeover_k`, `takeover_v` are two learned scalars per layer. The side state is whatever `intervention_source` selects, exactly as for the additive `kv` target: `"side_layers"` = the side layer s(l) = l input state at the rows (the matched-depth readout, the arms below), `"summary"` = the final normed state, `"passage_attention"` = either through the A2 block. Every target token's attention at that layer then reads, at the row slots, content the side model computed at that depth: encoder-decoder cross-attention with the row count as the slot budget.

**Why this initialisation.** A replacement cannot start as a no-op the way an additive delta does — whatever is written over the rows' K/V is what every later token reads at that layer, so a random slot wrecks the rows' keys from update 0 (the silent-phase failure of the scaled heads, now at the one place the reader cannot route around), and a side-computed value that is *meant* to reproduce the reader's own K/V would first have to learn a function of the reader's forward before it could help. The head therefore starts as a **residual around the reader's own value**: `down` is zero-initialised and both takeovers are 0, so the written K/V *are* the reader's own — bit-identical to no intervention and to the `interventions = 0` model, with no calibration state and no regression fit. From there the head owns its magnitude (no unit-RMS normalisation, no scale on the content — the lora-style parametrisation round 4 validated), and the takeover is what makes this a replacement rather than the additive `kv` target: an additive head can never cancel `K_reader[rows]` (a value it does not see), whereas a takeover of 1 discards it and the slot is purely side-computed. The two takeovers are the head's `scale_parameters()` (`ac_model_utils.py:690-692`) and train in the optimizer's scale group at `learning_rate_delta_scales` (`delta_scale_lr: 1e-3` in the arms; `ac_model_training.py:538`), like the scaled heads' relative scales: a scalar under Adam moves by about its rate per step, so at the encoder rate (1e-4) the sum over NKVSW's 1,024-update cosine schedule would bound |takeover| near 0.05 and the arm could never leave the reader's-own regime (review 6, finding 2), while at 1e-3 a full replacement (takeover 1) is reachable within the epoch. Starting from the safe value it cannot reproduce the round-3 scale collapse (there the scalar walked *away* from a harmful random delta; here it walks *toward* a replacement only when the loss asks for it). The keep factor is applied in fp32 and the written K/V cast once to the reader's dtype (`hf_utils.py:193-197`): in bf16, `1 − takeover` rounds to 1.0 below a takeover of 0.4 % and then moves in 0.8 % steps while the takeover's gradient is the exact linearisation, so the widget would show values with no forward effect (finding 4); at init (keep 1, content 0) either precision is exact. `intervention_takeover` plots the takeovers.

**Where it lives.** `ActivationContextModules` registers the slot heads under `delta_heads` (`ac_model.py:237-243`; the K/V calibration buffer `[L, 2]` as for `kv`), so every prefix-based mechanism (heads-only staging, `heads_frozen` refusal, the bench's shared-parameter hash, migration of an input-only checkpoint, the `optimizer.pt` layout) treats them exactly like the K/V delta heads. `ACOutput.kv_slots[layer] = (k, v, keep_k, keep_v)` (`ac_model_utils.py:499`); the trainer concatenates the parts' `k`/`v` and takes the keeps from the first part (they are the layer's, `ac_model_training.py:1244`). The payload key is `kv_slots` (`hf_utils.py:97`); the K/V hooks after `k_norm` and `v_proj` write `keep · own + content` over the row slices (`hf_utils.py:192-198`) and refuse a payload carrying both deltas and slots for one layer (`:176`). Everything else in the hook path is shared with `kv`: the pre-hook stashes the bound payload on the layer, the cache offset maps the positions, so `generate_with_interventions` binds the slots at prefill and the cache keeps them (tested: the first generated step's logits equal the training forward's on the prefix, greedy decoding equals a full recompute). `deltas_off` withholds the payload → the reader's own K/V (`ac_model_training.py:1222-1260`). Calibration measures the reader's K and V RMS at the rows as for `kv` (`ac_model.py:500`; the `rms_k`/`rms_v` buffers are diagnostic anchors only). Config validation: needs `intervention_head_style = "lora"` (a scaled head would replace the rows' keys with noise; `ac_model.py:158`), full-attention layers only (`:392`), `heads_frozen` refused (as for every lora head). `load()` refuses a checkpoint whose target differs (the existing `source/target` check).

**Diagnostics.** Every training forward the K/V hooks write the reader's own K/V RMS at the rows (`row_kv_rms`, as before) and the RMS of what was written (`slot_kv_rms`, `hf_utils.py:198`); the encoder records the slot content's RMS (`delta_rms`). The trace entry (`ac_model_training.py:1316-1341`) carries per layer and half `delta_rms` = content / own (the existing widget `intervention_delta_rms`), **`slot_rms` = written / own** (new widget `intervention_slot_rms`, exactly 1 at init) and `takeover` (new widget `intervention_takeover`, 0 at init); `scale`/`relative` are empty. `intervention_summary()` reports `takeover` per layer and `parametrization = "lora"`.

**vLLM mapping (the reason to prefer this shape).** The payload is, per request and per intervened layer, M rows of `(k, v)` — the same bytes as the additive K/V deltas (4,096 B per row per layer) — plus two scalars per layer that are AC-model state (versioned with the model, not per request). The application seam is the one `DEEP_ARCHITECTURES.md` §2.2 planned for B1, `Qwen3NextAttention.forward` after `_project_qkv_gate` and before `self.attn`, with the add replaced by a write: `k[idx] = keep_k · k[idx] + rot(slot_k)`, `v[idx] = keep_v · v[idx] + slot_v` (RoPE is linear, so rotating the slot content and mixing post-RoPE equals mixing pre-RoPE). It is a plain per-request write into the row positions' cache entries: no scheduler, block-hash or slot-mapping change beyond the planned per-block payload hash, chunked prefill and preemption/recompute hold because the payload is immutable, and at takeover 1 the engine may skip computing the rows' K/V altogether (a K/V prefix injection). Cost per step: one scalar multiply and one `index_add_`-class op on `[rows, 1024]` per layer — the same as the deltas.

## B. `intervention_target = "cross_attention"` — a ceiling measurement, not a candidate

At each chosen layer, **every target position** from the first row on gets a residual delta from a cross-attention block whose **queries** are the reader's own residual at that position and whose **keys and values** are the side model's final normed states at **all passage positions** (zero-init out projection, 4 heads of 160 at r = 640 on the 4B; `PassageAttention(d_target, r, d_passage=d_side, residual=False)`, `ac_model.py:247`). This is zero compression: the reader reads the whole passage's side states at every token, so it bounds what any fetch-side design at these layers can reach. It is HF-only and **not vLLM-compatible as is** (the whole passage travels with the request and a per-layer attention over it runs for every token, including decode steps); the arm notes and `intervention_summary()["measurement_only"]` say so.

Mechanics: the encoder emits `ACOutput.cross_passage` = the side model's final states at the passage (content) positions of the part (`ac_model.py:744`; the same tensor A2's depth "final" uses as keys; `detach_source` detaches it), no per-layer head. `_assemble_student(..., ac_model=)` builds `cross_inputs = {passage [N, d_side], visible_from [N], blocks {layer: block}}` (`ac_model_training.py:1251`; `visible_from` is each part's row-run start, so a target position reads only the parts whose rows precede it — causal with respect to the document; the model is required because the reader runs the blocks). The layer pre-hook runs the block on this chunk's positions from the first visible one on with a `[L', N]` boolean mask and adds the result to the layer input (`hf_utils.py:247-262`); at decode steps the single position is ≥ the first row, so generation gets the delta at every step (tested against a full recompute). A checkpointed layer recomputes the block on the same payload tensors, so direct and checkpointed gradients agree (tested), and the side adapter receives gradient through the passage states. `deltas_off` withholds the payload → the base forward (tested equal to the input-only model's panel). Diagnostics: the hook writes `cross_rms[layer] = [delta RMS, residual RMS at the positions written]`; the trace's `delta_rms` is their ratio, `attention_out_norm` / `attention_grad_norm` come from the blocks (the existing `intervention_attention` widget), `head_grad_norm` is the block's norm. The blocks live under `cross_attention.` (`INTERVENTION_MODULE_PREFIXES`, `ac_model.py:84`), so heads-only staging, the frozen control's refusal, the bench hash and migration treat them as the intervention modules. `payload_bytes` counts the whole passage (the honest cost). Config validation: `intervention_source` must be `"summary"` (the keys are the final passage states), `heads_frozen` refused.

## F. What was left designed-only

**`intervention_slots_extra` (extra K/V slots per layer beyond the row positions).** Refused when non-zero (`ac_model.py:156`). In HF the K/V hooks see `[B, S, H, D]` after `k_norm` / `v_proj`; lengthening the key/value axis by E entries per layer would have to extend the attention mask (or, under SDPA's `is_causal`, re-align it bottom-right), the RoPE positions of the extra entries and the `DynamicCache` layer's sequence length, per layer — the "virtual KV slots" D1 of the survey, rejected there for the same reasons on the engine side (every vLLM structure that touches KV is indexed by token position: `num_tokens`, block hashes, `slot_mapping`, `seq_lens`, positions). The mechanism that maps to the same per-request K/V write is **more rows**: E extra placeholder positions per part rendered next to the rows, whose input embeddings are a learned constant and whose K/V at the intervened layers are slot content like the rows'. That is `num_view_rows + E` with a constant input row — the row count is already the bench's ratio knob, so no separate switch was built. Nothing else was left out: both targets are wired through payload, deltas-off, trace, widgets, calibration, checkpoints, generation, the bench and the tests.

## Deviations / decisions

1. **The takeover scalars are the one addition to "a residual around the reader's own value"** (two parameters per layer, in the heads' prefix, trained in the scale group at `learning_rate_delta_scales`): without them the replacement is mechanically the additive lora-style K/V head (NKVWL), and the arm would be a replicate. With them `kv_slots ⊇ kv` and the arm pair NKVSW / NKVWL isolates replacement vs addition. Being scale parameters they are taken out of the encoder group before the gate candidates are formed, so they never join a `learning_rate_gates` group (the ND recipe leaves `gate_lr` unset anyway).
2. **The slot head requires the lora style** rather than silently ignoring `head_style`, so the arm declares it and the checkpoint's `head_style` stays meaningful.
3. **`cross_attention` blocks live in `ActivationContextModules.cross_attention` and travel in the payload as module references** (`cross_inputs["blocks"]`), so the reader hook runs the AC model's own parameters; records and the row cache never carry modules (the cache stores `cross_passage` only; the trainer attaches the blocks at assembly). `_assemble_student` therefore takes `ac_model=` (keyword-only; every call site passes it; a cross-attention output without it raises).
4. **Causality of the ceiling**: a target position attends only the passages of parts whose row run starts at or before it (`visible_from`); positions before the first row are not written. This keeps the measurement faithful to the document order (no token reads content that came later in the uncompressed sequence).
5. Not done: no GPU run, no vLLM change (the payload shape for slots is the additive K/V payload plus two per-layer scalars of model state; the cross-attention target has no engine mapping by design).

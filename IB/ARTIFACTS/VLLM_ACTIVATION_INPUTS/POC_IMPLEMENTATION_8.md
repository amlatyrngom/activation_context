## What it does

**A. `encoder_passes` (model config, default 1 = today, bit-identical).** The side decoder reads `[content; summaries]` `encoder_passes` times. Pass k ≥ 2 keeps the content positions and replaces the summary inputs by `unit_rows(summaries_1 + refine_adapter(unit_rows(rows_{k−1}))) × input_scale`, where `summaries_1` is the pass-1 summary block (pooled unit rows + marker + positions, before its unit normalisation), `rows_{k−1}` the side model's final states at the row positions of the previous pass, and `refine_adapter` a `d_side → min(256, d_side) → d_side` bottleneck (`up(silu(down(x)))`) whose output projection is zero-initialised — so pass 2 is an exact re-read at init and a warm start from any single-pass checkpoint stays on the control line. The mixer and pooling run once; the rows (`target_head`) and, for the K/V-transfer and layer-matched models, the captured side K/V and states come from the **last** pass only (B1). Gradient runs through every pass; each pass is its own (non-reentrant-checkpointed) decoder call. Within-run readouts (B3): a `passes_off` held-out panel (the reporting items re-scored with `encoder_passes` forced to 1: held − passes_off is the later passes' worth at inference) and the `refine_rms` (RMS of the adapter's contribution over the RMS of `summaries_1`) and `pass_cosine` (pass-1 vs last-pass unit rows) traces on the training forwards and on the panel.

**B′. `rare_weight` (trainer config, default 0 = bit-identical).** The SFT cross-entropy of the training forwards is weighted per target token by `w_t = 1 + rare_weight · s_t / mean(s)`, normalised to average 1 over the example, where `s_t = −log p_base(t | stripped history)` is the base model's no-context surprisal of target token t — the per-token log-prob the trainer already stores for the no-context reference (now built for every example with rows whenever the surprisal features are on, whatever the penalty's setting). The recorded and reported losses stay unweighted; evaluation forwards and the penalty examples are untouched.

**C. `pool_surprisal` (model config, default 0 = bit-identical).** The summary pooling's bins are drawn by cumulative mass `1 + pool_surprisal · s_t / mean(s)` over the content tokens instead of token count (`mass_bins`: bin j spans the tokens holding the mass points `[j T/V, (j+1) T/V]`, with the same floor/ceil boundary semantics as today's bins — a uniform mass reproduces them exactly; every bin is nonempty, consecutive bins overlap by at most one token, their union is every token; a bin is at most `(1 + pool_surprisal) ×` the uniform width + 2 tokens, so the `[V, width, d]` gather stays bounded), and the pooling attention logits gain the bias `pool_surprisal · b_h · s_t / mean(s)` with `b_h` a zero-initialised per-head parameter (`summary_pooling.surprisal_bias`, registered only when on). Rows per part are unchanged. `s_t` here is the side **base** model's (adapter disabled) no-context surprisal of the part's side tokens, computed inside the encoder by one extra no-grad side forward per uncached part (chunked gold-log-prob readout through the LM head; `s_0 = 0`; nested parts' placeholder runs get the mean) and cached by the part's token ids — it works at write time (rollout) as in training, and it aligns exactly with what is pooled. The `pool_bins` diagnostics (mean base surprisal of the tokens in a bin — its row mean, the first row's and the last row's — the widest bin and the uniform width) are traced on the training forwards and on the panel.

**Panel `rare_token_loss` / `rare_token_accuracy`** (surprisal runs only): per held-out item, over the target tokens whose stored base surprisal is in the item's top 20 % (`ceil(0.2 · C)` tokens, at least one, `torch.topk` ties), the student's mean −log p and teacher-forced accuracy; item means per set and per kind. The held-out items' references are stored once per `train()` call (one no-grad base forward per reporting / validation item).

### Alignment of the stored surprisal with the pooled tokens (the coordinator's check)

The stored reference is the base's log-prob of every **target** token of the **reader's** tokenisation of the stripped history (`_store_teacher_targets(..., lora=None)` on `example.penalty`), indexed exactly like `target_ids` — so it is the right quantity for B′ (a per-target-token weight) and for the panel, with no alignment work. The encoder pools the **side** tokenisation of the part (the chat-template wrapper plus the passage), whose positions do not map onto the target positions one for one (different wrapper, offsets, and in general a different side model); mapping the stored values onto the side tokens by matching runs would be fragile. So C computes the surprisal it needs where it needs it, from the side base over the very ids that are pooled, at the cost of one no-grad side forward per uncached part (≈ a third of a side pass; cached by ids, so a multi-epoch run pays once per part) — and the two features stay independent of each other's bookkeeping.

## Decisions / what is not done

1. **Cost of a pass (the review's cost line).** Pass 2 recomputes the N content positions, whose states are identical to pass 1 (causal: content never attends to summaries) — ≈ 97 % of the second forward is redundant at 1/32. Training side cost × `encoder_passes` (forward and backward; the reader's cost is unchanged, so the step grows by roughly the side share, ≈ +30–40 % at passes 2 on the arms), write-time (rollout) cost × `encoder_passes` per uncached part, inference cost at the reader identical. The production form is a cache-continued pass over the V summary positions only (K/V cache + GDN recurrent state snapshotted after the content, graph-attached under non-reentrant checkpointing) — not this round.
2. **The refinement is added before the unit normalisation** (`unit_rows(summaries_1 + refine(prev)) × input_scale`, the same normalisation and scale the pass-1 summaries get) and is itself neither unit-normalised nor scaled by a learned scalar (the lora-style lesson of finding 18); `refine_rms` reads its magnitude relative to `summaries_1`.
3. **Surprisal source for C is the side base, computed in the encoder**, not the stored reference (the alignment note above). Cost: one no-grad side forward per uncached part, cached by ids (`surprisal_cache`, up to 100k parts); at write time the same. `s_0 = 0`; the chunked readout is fp32 through `head_logits`.
4. **`pool_surprisal` is both the switch and the strength**: the mass is `1 + γ · s/mean(s)` (γ = 0: uniform bins, bit-identical; γ = 1: the token-count prior and the surprisal contribute equally, bounding a bin at 2 × the uniform width + 2), and the logit bias is `γ · b_h · s/mean(s)`. At γ > 0 the bins move at init, so the pooling arms start off the control line by the bin change alone (the bias starts at zero); this is the feature.
5. **The rare-token panel exists on surprisal runs only** (`rare_weight > 0` or `pool_surprisal > 0`), because its references cost one base forward per held-out item and the off path must stay bit-identical; the review's post-hoc script over finished arms' `result.json` (every target token's log-prob is stored per item) is the way to get the controls' number and is not written here.
6. **The passes_off panel is automatic** when `encoder_passes > 1` and reporting data exist (no new trainer key); it re-encodes every reporting item once more at one pass per panel.
7. **Memory under `pool_surprisal`**: the pooling gather is `[V, width, d]` with `width ≤ (1 + γ) N/V + 2`; the surprisal forward's transient logits are chunked (256 positions × vocab, fp32).
8. **Not done**: no GPU run (the bf16 autocast path of the pooling bias — an additive float `attn_mask` next to a float key-padding mask on `nn.MultiheadAttention` — ran on CPU fp32 only); no vLLM change; no bidirectional-summary or second-block variants (review §1); no per-class split of the rare-token panel (digit / identifier piece / other); the cache-continued second pass; a `pool_surprisal` sweep beyond γ = 1 is a bench decision, not code.

# Null review — dense supervision (round 7, results finding 21)

Read-only adversarial review of the nine-arm null. Sources: `DENSE_SUPERVISION_PLAN.md`, `DESIGN_REVIEW_7.md`, `POC_IMPLEMENTATION_7.md`, `POC_REVIEW_9.md`, `poc_changes_7.patch`; the applied code (`activation/ac_model/ac_model_training.py:445–453, 720–754, 819–821, 1226–1232, 1289–1492, 1527–1549`; `activation/harness/hf_utils.py:410–511`; `module_manager.py:190–220`); `activation/tests/test_distill.py` (re-run here: 7 passed, 45 s); the logged runs `IB/TMP/SYNC/AC_RECON/{A32XDn, A32XDcn, NXDn, A32KVTADn}` against `{A32X, WCn, A32KVTAn, A32P2n}` (`training_stats.json`, `report_data.json`, `result.json`). All nine arms share `seed 20260915`, so the same-init arms see the same examples in the same order and their step-1 gradients are computed on identical parameters (determinism floor: A32X vs A32P2n step-1 encoder norm 1.624 vs 1.631).

## Verdict: NULL IS REAL for the objective as implemented — NULL IS CONFOUNDED as a test of the idea

No implementation artefact was found: λ is applied as configured, the term reaches the encoder and the reader LoRA at a third to a half of the terminal gradient's norm, the teacher is the un-adapted base on the full-context view, the positions are aligned, and the term is far from negligible (it costs 0.03–0.04 nats of terminal training loss while λ = 1). What the runs show is that *this* target — the base model's attention output — is outside the warm reader's representational frame and is unsatisfiable at 1/32, so the term pulls the rows and the reader toward hedged, base-like outputs that the terminal loss undoes as soon as λ decays. That is a real null for "regress the warm reader's attention outputs onto the base's", not evidence about dense per-layer supervision in the reader's own frame.

## Ranked list of ways a null could arise even if the idea works

**1. LIKELY (design) — wrong teacher frame: the base teacher is a different model from the warm LoRA reader.** At update 0 the row-fed reader's attention outputs are *further* from the base's than the same reader with no rows at layers 11–31: rows-on / rows-off = 1.13/1.10, 1.18/1.06, 1.15/0.93, 1.14/0.88, 1.02/0.82, 0.80/0.68 (A32XDn; identical values in NXDn's and A32KVTADn's rows-off, same K16 reader, same 8 items) — while the rows cut the terminal loss from 1.89 to 0.95. The K16 reader reads rows through a mechanism whose attention-output signature does not resemble the base's passage reading, so "match the base" and "read the rows" disagree: (a) with the same data order the terminal training loss is worse under the term within ten updates (u = 9–12: 0.760/0.741/0.749/0.753 vs 0.750/0.727/0.736/0.722) and by 0.038 over the first quarter (0.448 vs 0.410; λ 0.25: 0.415); held-out behind the control by 0.02–0.035 at 0.25–0.5 epochs; (b) the rows-on term is λ-dependent, not monotone: it falls to 0.64 (layer 11) by u = 385 and climbs back to 0.71 by u = 1024 as λ decays (layer 27: 0.70 → 0.81; layer 31: 0.43 → 0.48), whereas the constant-λ twin keeps falling (0.79 → 0.75) — the terminal loss actively pulls the outputs away from the base's; (c) the rows' attention share at u = 128 is lower at every layer under λ = 1 than under λ = 0.25 (0.13/0.14/0.25/0.42/0.50/0.44/0.44/0.21 vs 0.16/0.18/0.29/0.46/0.53/0.51/0.47/0.24) and rises only after λ has decayed (layer 11: 0.23 at u = 512 → 0.29 at 1024) — the term rewards attending *less* to the base-unlike rows. NXDn (1/16, converged init) is the sharpest case: held-out 0.190 → 0.231 at 0.125 epochs (control 0.188). Nothing here needs a bug; it needs a teacher whose outputs live where the student's do.

**2. LIKELY (design) — an MSE on activations in a lossy regime is a hedging objective, and on this material it carries no information the terminal loss lacks.** The best any arm reaches is 0.55–0.85 at layers 7–27 (the residual is the passage content 1/32 rows cannot carry); the MSE optimum given the rows is the conditional mean of the teacher's output, a hedged vector that the warm downstream layers were not trained to consume. The tell is that the gain in the term is not retained: every arm's terminal loss returns exactly to the control once λ is at the floor (A32XDn +0.007, A32XDcn +0.006, NXDn +0.004, A32KVTADn +0.003; A32XDL −0.000 after 5,472 updates), so what the rows learned under the term was hedging, not content. On reconstruction material the teacher's attention output at target position t is essentially the induction fetch of token t + 1 — the same information as the terminal target, which is already supervised at every token; the term adds the base's *format*, not a new signal.

**3. PLAUSIBLE (design) — the term cannot open a channel the reader does not read.** Its gradient into the row content passes through the same attention weights as the terminal loss. On the transfer arm the target positions put 0.00–0.07 of their attention on the transferred rows at init, and the term's step-1 gradient into the encoder is nil (A32KVTADn 0.813 vs A32KVTAn 0.812; reader +2 %); the rows-on term only starts moving once the terminal loss has opened the channel (share 0.12–0.35 at u = 128). The plan's "write nothing is no longer stationary" holds only where the reader already listens.

**4. PLAUSIBLE — reader-side "row carrier" shortcut not separable from encoder progress.** Rows-off is flat through every run (≤ 0.07 movement), so the B1 shortcut (fitting a rows-independent mean) did not happen; but the reader's v/o LoRA at the row positions can reshape *existing* row content into base-like outputs without the encoder writing more, and the panel cannot distinguish that from better rows. A32XDR (reader 5e-5, null) says the reader's rate is not the lever, not that the path was the rows.

**5. PLAUSIBLE-low — normalisation.** Per-layer `mean_pos ‖A_t‖²` is the right scale for `attn_out`, but if the post-`o_proj` output has a few massive dimensions the term is a magnitude match on those (unchecked here; no model on this box). Equal layer weights are harmless (layer 3 sits at 0.13–0.17 and contributes little gradient).

**6. Minor — joint clipping.** Every step is clipped (`max_grad_norm 1.0`, pre-clip norms 2.5–4.3); with the term in the norm the terminal component of the step shrinks by ≈ 15–20 % during the hold. Explains part of the hold-phase lag, not the final null (the constant-λ 0.25 arm is null too).

**7. EXCLUDED — λ not applied / wrong clock.** Logged λ: 1.0 through u = 257, 0.998 at 258, 0.918 at 301, 0.527 at 512, 0.052 at 768, 0.05 after; 0.25 flat for the `c` arms. The clock starts at 0 (LR trace 2e-6 at u = 1 = warm-up step 1/50; `updates_done` not inherited from the init), `planned_total` = 1,024, no truncation (1024/1024 updates in 42 min of a 27,000 s budget). `_distill_lambda_at` (`:1387`) matches the plan.

**8. EXCLUDED — gradient cut before the writer.** Same-seed step-1 norms (identical parameters, identical 8 examples): encoder 1.624 (control) → 1.953 (λ 1) → 1.698 (λ 0.25) ⇒ ‖g_dense‖ ≈ 0.51 (32 % of the terminal's) at cos ≈ +0.55; reader 3.105 → 3.815 → 3.244 ⇒ ‖g_dense‖ ≈ 1.37 (44 %), cos ≈ +0.36. The hook is on `self_attn` (output after `o_proj`, LoRA included, `hf_utils.py:452`), the capture is a basic slice + clone (saves nothing; the recompute runs after the handles are removed, and `if record[index]: return` guards a second fire); the term is added to the objective before `backward` with the example's weight and share (`:744–747`). Test 3 (term alone → encoder and reader LoRA grads, checkpointed = direct at 1e-6; input-only and kv_transfer) passes here.

**9. EXCLUDED — teacher sees rows or adapters, or the wrong history.** `lora_context(model, None)` calls `enable_adapters(False)` on every `BaseTunerLayer` (`module_manager.py:207–212`); `_distill_teacher` is `@torch.no_grad`, `gradient_checkpointing=False`, no payload (`:1401–1418`); the test asserts `torch.equal` with a plain base forward and invariance to a LoRA perturbation. The teacher view carries the passage: teacher tokens 5.06 M vs student 2.82 M with 2.32 M completion tokens ⇒ ≈ 2.31 M passage tokens present only in the teacher (1 − 1/32 of the gap). Rows-off panel values at u = 0 are bit-identical across the three K16 arms.

**10. EXCLUDED — misaligned positions.** `_check_target_alignment` (`:1353–1362`) asserts, for every training example and for the panel's rows-off twin, equal-length per-token position lists and matching (predicting token, target token) pairs; a mismatch raises at `build_example`, and the runs completed.

**11. EXCLUDED — term numerically negligible.** Term 0.8–1.1 at u = 1 vs terminal 0.87 (parity as designed); the training loss diverges from the control's within ten updates; the panel divergence during the hold is 4–8 × the warm-start noise floor (0.01).

**12. EXCLUDED — schedule or length as the sole lever.** λ 1 hold/decay, λ 0.25 constant, 1,024 and 5,472 updates, reader 2e-5 and 5e-5: all null. λ 3 (A32XD3) was cancelled, not run; given that λ 1 already costs terminal loss during the hold, a larger λ tests the cost, not the idea.

## Answers to the six questions

1. λ: applied exactly as configured; the clock is the LR clock from 0 (item 7).
2. Gradient: reaches the encoder and the reader LoRA on the input-only arms (item 8); on the transfer arm it reaches the encoder only after the reader has started attending to the transferred rows (item 3).
3. Teacher and alignment: correct (items 9, 10). But "correct" is the problem: the base is not the student's frame (item 1).
4. Magnitude: not negligible (item 11). `distill_loss` fell from 1.0–1.1 to 0.55–0.85 at layers 7–27 (layer 31: 0.77 → 0.43; layer 3: 0.17 → 0.13) within ~100 updates, mostly through the rows (rows-off flat, rows-on − rows-off gap 0 → 0.3–0.4), then rose again as λ decayed while the terminal loss caught up with the control. A term that decreases while the terminal loss lags, and re-increases while the terminal loss recovers, is a term in conflict with the terminal objective, not one starved of gradient.
5. Long enough / λ large enough: yes to reject this objective on a warm reader (item 12); not a test of the idea with a teacher in the student's frame or of a non-hedging (distributional or cosine) target.
6. Confounds: the warm reader's own mechanism (item 1), MSE hedging on a lossy channel (item 2), the shared attention path (item 3), the un-attributable reader-side carrier (item 4), clipping (item 6). Which parameters can reduce the loss: the encoder (rows), the reader LoRA at row positions (v/o) and at target positions (q, o), and by attending less to the rows. The observed early drop came with a *falling* attention share and a rising terminal loss — the cheapest path was not "rows carry more".

## The settling diagnostic

Rerun A32XDn unchanged except **teacher = the reader's own init adapter (a frozen copy of the K16 reader LoRA) on the full-context view** — a fixed, student-independent target in the student's frame (no shortcut: the copy never moves) — with two widgets: (i) at u = 0, the panel's rows-on vs rows-off with both teachers (if rows-on < rows-off only with the same-frame teacher, item 1 is proven: the base target lay outside what the rows deliver); (ii) per reporting boundary, the cosine between the encoder's dense-term gradient and its terminal gradient on the panel items (two extra backward calls over eight items). Read: same-frame arm below A32X by more than 0.01 with cos ≥ 0 ⇒ the round-7 null was the teacher; still level with cos ≤ 0 ⇒ the null is real for dense attention-output supervision on a warm reader. A cheaper attribution arm if a slot is short: reader frozen (`reader_lr 0`) + the same-frame term, so any gap must come from the rows.

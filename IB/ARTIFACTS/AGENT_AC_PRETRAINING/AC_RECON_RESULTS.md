# AC reconstruction night — results

Draft in progress (updated 12:50 UTC 09-16); final numbers land as the runs finish. Protocol: `ac_recon/PROTOCOL.md`; every run with its hypothesis, expectation and outcome: `ac_recon/LEDGER.md`; live pages: `IB/TMP/SYNC/AC_RECON/<label>/report.tressoir.html`.

## Setting

Task: reproduce a 400–2000-character passage (tool outputs from SWE and deep-research trajectories, text the model has not memorised) from activation-context rows written over placeholders in the reader's input, plain assistant text, SFT on the passage tokens. Held panel: 200 novel passages (split by trajectory) and 100 Wikipedia passages (memorisation detector). The metric is gold NLL per passage token with the rows; the **no-context floor** is the same reader on the same items without rows (base model: 1.9209, identical to the initial reader's), and the **honest gap** is floor minus novel NLL — what the rows contribute beyond whatever the reader learned about the domain.

Recipe under test: encoder LoRA LR 1e-4, reader LoRA LR 2e-5 with the no-context penalty `λ·KL(base ‖ reader)` at the target positions of the stripped history, λ = 1 on a 25 % sample scaled by 1/p, top-32 stored base targets, 8 examples per update, 2 epochs. Anchors: A16 (ratio 1/16) and A4 (1/4) at 21,888 passages (7.5 h budget). Side arms change one variable each; half-size arms use 10,944 passages.

## Findings (updated 17:00 UTC; K16, A4K, R4X, K16r1/r2, ND/NDC/NDI final; X40K / K16D / K16G0 / A32K / X40KD still training; every number below is on the full 200-item panel — see finding 9 for the clean-subset correction)

1. **Anchors.** A16 (ratio 1/16, 21,888 passages × 2 epochs, 5,472 updates of 8): held novel gold NLL 1.905 → **0.878**, wiki 2.338 → 1.349, token accuracy 0.78, no-context floor 1.921 → 1.875, seen-training 0.740. A4 (1/4, same recipe): **1.506** / 2.265 / floor 1.873 — no transition in 5,472 updates. Each took 7.2 h on one RTX PRO 6000 at one example per forward.
2. **The silent phase: the row scale climbs, but it is a symptom, not the cause.** Every run descends slowly for 1.5–3k updates, then sharply (grad norm 1.6 → 10–40, clipped). The AC modules' scalar gates sit in the encoder's 1e-4 Adam group, and Adam moves a scalar by about its rate per step. `target_head.scale` (the rows' magnitude relative to the reader's embeddings) starts at the embedding RMS 0.0128 and R0F's checkpoints show 0.080 after epoch 1 and 0.191 after epoch 2. Giving the gates their own group at 3e-3 did not shorten the phase: on the micro node the scale jumped to 0.03 within 25 updates and stayed there (the gradient does not ask for more until the encoder has content to send), and G16 (gates at 3e-3, otherwise A16) trailed A16 through epoch 0.375 (1.544 vs 1.471). The 15× climb happens *after* the transition. What does move the onset: **the row head's initialization.** The head was a random d→4d→d FFN whose RMS-normalized output the reader saw as random unit vectors at step 0. With an identity skip (rows = RMS-normalized (x + FFN(x)), FFN output zero-initialized, so the rows start as the side model's own last hidden state — with tied embeddings that is already a vector in the space the reader's embedding matrix speaks) the transition happens inside the first 500 updates: K16 (otherwise G1b's recipe) reached 1.105 at epoch 0.125 where A16 and G1b sat at 1.57, **0.294 at epoch 1.0 and a final 0.190 (clean subset 0.188; wiki 0.310; token accuracy 0.94; floor drift 0.027)** — A16 finished at 0.878 (clean 0.922), G1b at 0.654 (clean 0.691) — and it was still falling at the end (K16r1/K16r2, other seeds on ORCD, sit at 0.49 and 0.71 at epoch 0.625, so the onset varies by data order but the transition is 3/3), and at S size SK beat SC 1.423 vs 1.621 in 128 updates with equal floors. The silent phase was the encoder learning to make a random projection legible. The 0.25 level on tool-output text (perplexity 1.29) says most of that text is structure the reader predicts from a few cues; the wiki panel (0.396 at 1.125) is the honest prose number.
3. **The penalty works as a drift cap; a free reader is worse anyway.** Without it (P0, half size) the reader's no-context floor fell 1.921 → 1.689 and its novel NLL (0.912) was *worse* than the penalised reader's (B: 0.770, floor 1.882). Drift under the penalty is ≈ 0.04–0.05 nats per run at λ = 1 and at λ = 3. **Gold-inclusive reference targets** (the base's top-32 plus the gold token, closing the KL's blind spot on mass moved onto gold tokens outside the top 32) are the best single change seen: G1b, otherwise A16's recipe, reached 0.745 at epoch 1.625 (A16: 0.905 at 1.875), with the same floor drift.
4. **The encoder is the channel; the 1/4 failure was the random row head, not the trainable reader.** Reader frozen: R0F (1/16) 0.880 with the floor pinned at 1.921 (honest gap 1.04, fidelity 0.375, no train/held gap); R0F4 (1/4) transitioned at ~0.7 epoch, reached 0.772 at epoch 1.625 and ended at 0.906 (the encoder alone overfits late at 1/4). So 1/4 is not a capacity or marker problem: the same rows work when the reader stays put. The same data with a trainable reader (A4) never left 1.5 — but that is a single run, and finding 5 shows a run with an identical recipe can fail to transition by chance (R0F3). A plausible mechanism, undemonstrated: before the transition a trainable reader lowers its loss by discounting near-random rows, weakening the gradient that reaches the encoder, and four times as many uninformative rows make that attractor stronger. **Resolved by A4K** (1/4, trainable reader, gold targets + skip-init head): novel **0.962 at epoch 0.125** (426 updates) and 1.266 at 0.25, where A4 sat at 1.49 at 0.375 and never moved — with rows the reader can read from step 0 there is nothing to route around, and the same trainable reader transitions at 1/4 immediately. R4X (frozen reader, 1/4, 40k passages) transitioned between epochs 0.75 and 0.875 (1.177 → 0.795) and sits at 0.80 at 1.125. A4G (A4 + gold targets, no skip) was still at 1.489 at 0.375 when killed: gold targets alone do not fix 1/4; the head init does. Staging (reader frozen for the first half, F1, half size) did not help at 1/16: 1.056 vs B's 0.770.
5. **Replicate variance in the onset is large.** R0F and R0F3 (same recipe, same data order, 2 vs 3 epochs) transitioned at ~3k updates and never (8,208 updates) respectively; B and B4 differ likewise. Finding 2 explains part of it (the scalar's walk to audibility is noisy). Single-run comparisons of onset time are therefore weak evidence; end-of-run levels after the transition are more stable (0.77–0.91 for every 1/16 arm that transitioned).
6. **More passes help, so far without a penalty on held.** B4 (10,944 × 4 epochs) reached **0.559** at epoch 3.25 (best held number of the night; OOM'd in epoch 4, checkpoint of epoch 3 saved) vs B's 0.770 after 2 epochs and A16's 0.878 after one pass over twice the data. Unique data vs repeats at equal updates: X40 (40,000 × 2) is the test in flight.
7. **Harmful or neutral:** encoder LR 3e-4 (E3/E3F), cued continuation (C5), λ = 3 (P3), penalty on every example (P1F: 1.50 at epoch 1.375), reader LR 5e-5 (RL5: 1.015 at 1.375; RL1 at 1e-5 was ahead of A16 at 0.796 at 1.25 when node 2 was drained). Ratio 1/8 (A8) sits between 1/16 and 1/4 (1.063 at 1.25).
8. **Throughput.** 3 examples per padded forward: 1.58 s/update (5 examples/s, ~3.5k tokens/s forward+backward, 64 GB). 8 per forward with checkpointing above 3,000 padded tokens: 2.3 s/update (padding + recompute), so 3 stays. The batch of the three longest passages plus the penalty's second forward OOMs at 96 GB without checkpointing below 1,700 tokens (B4, E3F, G1, G3 lost).

9. **The held "novel" panel was 12.5 % contaminated (code review 2, 12:40 UTC).** The same file is viewed in several trajectories with different random window cuts; the trajectory split and the exact-window hash missed it. With 50-character shingles, 25/200 held items share > 50 % of their text with a training passage on the 40k material (43/200 on the 100k material used by X40K/R4X; wiki 0), and the leaked items are intrinsically easier (base floor 1.60 vs 1.94). Re-scored on the clean 151 items (`ac_recon/clean_panel.py`): A16 0.878 → **0.922**, G1b 0.654 → **0.691**, B 0.770 → 0.807, R0F 0.880 → 0.922, P0 0.912 → 0.958, A4 1.506 → 1.589 — every number moves by ~0.04 and no ranking changes. From here on headlines are quoted clean / all; a deduplicated material (`material_40k_clean.json`: 35,212 training passages after dropping 4,788 near-duplicates, 152 clean held items) is used for the ORCD headline run X40KD. The training pool's own near-duplication (12 % at the > 50 % level in keep-first order) also weakens the "unique data vs repeats" premise of X40 vs B4.
10. **Code review 2 (two independent reviewers, 12:15–12:45 UTC; `ac_recon/CODE_REVIEW_2_{fork,fresh}.md`)** added, behind flags: cosine LR decay with a real warm-up (the constant 1e-4 explains the late-run swings of 0.05–0.15 nats: R0F4 0.772 → 0.906, R4X 1.005 → 1.177 → 0.795), per-group gradient clipping (the joint 1.0 clip was binding at every step and scaled the reader's step by the encoder's transition spikes), an exact penalty count per update (Binomial(8, 0.25) gave 10 % of updates no penalty and 11 % four or more), token-weighted loss and metric, a row-scale init multiplier, a gate group holding only the row scale, no-mixer, and a live full-vocabulary penalty (the stored top-32 reference leaves the tail free; the gold-token fix that was worth 0.22 nats closed one hole of it). Also fixed: a resumed run inherited the checkpoint's learning rates; and 96 % of example-building time was `len(tokenizer)` recomputing the vocabulary size (3 calls × 31 ms per example — 2.1 h for X40K's 80k examples; now once per tokenizer). The flags are being decided by ten midi arms (2,048 × 2) on ORCD and two paired anchor-size runs on node 1 (K16D: K16 + decay + per-group clip; K16G0: K16 without gold targets).

11. **What the remaining errors are (K16 final panel, 14:10 UTC; `ac_recon/deviations.py`).** With a deviation defined as a target token whose teacher-forced log-prob is below −1 nat (p < 0.37), 6.4 % of the 72,756 panel tokens deviate (novel 5.6 %, wiki 9.8 %). By token class the rate is prose words 8.6 %, identifier / sub-word pieces 7.2 %, numbers 5.7 %, punctuation and markup 4.6 %, whitespace and indentation 2.4 %. Syntax is what the reader gets right (indentation, brackets, template markers, line prefixes, code structure); the misses are content: gist-preserving paraphrase in prose ("electrical" → "electronic", "superlattice" → "superstructure", "Karl Wilhelm Illiger" → "Karl Illiger"), plausible-but-wrong specifics (1798 → 1778, DOI/URL digits, counts), and recombined identifier parts (`_render_for_list_str` → `_render_list_for_str`, reused consistently across all six calls of a sample). Under teacher forcing the errors stay local (mean −logp of the 8 tokens after a deviation 0.285 vs 0.232 overall); in free generation a miss on a content token derails the rest of the line. The 1/16 rows behave like a lossy semantic summary: structure and gist recoverable, exact wording mostly, high-entropy specifics (digits, ids, name order) lost — which is why wiki (denser in names and numbers) trails novel, and why a RAG-QA transfer check should separate date/identifier answers from descriptive ones.

12. **The 1/4 gap is optimization, not capacity (A4X, 16:50 UTC).** The AC modules are ratio-free, so K16r1's adapters (trained only at 1/16, held 0.210) were evaluated at ratio 1/4 with no training: **0.225** — against A4K's 0.364 trained from scratch at 1/4 (5,472 updates) and A4's 1.506 with a random row head. Fine-tuned at 1/4 on the ND schedule (8,192 × 1, cosine) it reaches **0.079 at epoch 0.875 with teacher-forced token accuracy 0.977** (the 1/16 warm-start control WCb on the same schedule: 0.139), so denser rows carry more once the channel exists; going sparser transfers less (A32X: 0.946 untrained at 1/32 → 0.343 @0.75). Denser rows are read almost losslessly by a 1/16-trained encoder/reader; from-scratch 1/4 training is slower to find the channel and still unconverged after two epochs (A4K oscillated 0.6–0.95 for most of the run). Consequence: train at 1/16 and change the ratio at deployment, or warm-start denser ratios from a 1/16 checkpoint. R4X (1/4, reader frozen at 1/4 trainable) 0.401 says the reader is not the bottleneck either.
13. **Same-recipe variance by run size.** 2,048 × 2 (512 updates): onset or no onset (MD 0.800 vs MK 1.581). 8,192 × 1 (1,024 updates): ND 0.712 vs NDC 0.981 with the identical initial hash, data order and update count — ~0.27 nats. 21,888 × 2 (5,472 updates): K16 0.190 / K16r1 0.210 / K16r2 0.299 — ~0.11. The spread is onset timing; it shrinks with updates past the onset. Cross-arm claims at midi size need replicate pairs or a warm start from a converged checkpoint (protocol below); within-run controls (an eval panel with a component removed) are the robust readout.
14. **Deep interventions, first POC (NDI vs NDC, 16:45 UTC; `IB/ARTIFACTS/VLLM_ACTIVATION_INPUTS/`).** Five bottleneck heads (2560→640→2560) read the shared summary state and add a per-row residual delta before reader layers 7, 11, 19, 23, 31 (interior fifth-depth points snapped to full-attention blocks, plus the last block), RMS-normalised and scaled by a per-layer scalar initialised at 1 % of the calibrated residual RMS; zero mode is bit-identical to the previous code. ND recipe (8,192 × 1, cosine): **NDI 0.753 held novel / 1.049 wiki; the same model with the deltas removed at eval 0.799 / 1.121 — the deltas are worth 0.046 novel and 0.072 wiki at inference**, growing over the run (0.007 → 0.054; 290/300 panel items improved, median 0.050). NDC 0.981 is inside the midi-size spread (finding 13), so the cross-run gap is not evidence. The training review (`POC_TRAINING_REVIEW.md`) found the scales under-trained by construction (absolute scalars in the 1e-4 group: relative step lr/rms_l, 7.6× larger at layer 7 than 31; all five at 40–80 % of the Adam bound all run; layers 19–31 still LR-limited at the decay), so the measured worth tracked the allowed magnitude and "under-trained" vs "information-starved" cannot be separated yet. The architecture review (`DEEP_ARCHITECTURES.md`) ranks the next candidates: heads reading layer-matched side states (A1), per-layer passage attention in the encoder (A2), additive K/V deltas at the row positions of full-attention layers (B1), a shared gated module by `intervention_name` (C1); virtual KV slots, GDN-state prefixes and per-request low-rank weights are rejected. Next arms run warm-started from K16r1 (noise floor from WCa/WCb) with the relative-scale parametrisation.

## Engineering notes

- Batched SFT path (`micro_batch_examples`): 3 examples per right-padded reader forward, equivalent to the per-example path (tests; R0 vs R0F at equal updates), 2.2× faster; memory needs checkpointing for batches of 1,700+ tokens when the penalty's second forward is present (three OOMs at the batch holding the longest passages).
- Shared epoch-checkpoint folders between concurrent runs on a node (`SYNC/AC_MODELS/<ac>/epoch_NNN`): a mover loop on nodes 1–2, per-run roots for later runs. To fix in the trainer: a per-run checkpoint prefix.
- ssh to the nodes stalls when `SSH_AUTH_SOCK` points at a dead agent socket; unset it.
- Example building for 21,888 items took ~40 min of one core: 96 % of it was `len(tokenizer)` (fixed 12:45 UTC: now ~1 min). The ~30 min base-reference pass is shared per material under `preparation/cache/` and disappears with the live penalty.
- Resume: epoch and mid-epoch checkpoints carry `optimizer.pt`; the bench resumes from the newest record under the run's checkpoint root (verified live on ORCD Slurm with `--requeue` and chained 6-h jobs).

## 15. Deep interventions, warm-started: learned delta scales collapse to zero (18:22 UTC)

Phase F (relative scales = calibrated RMS × a learned relative factor, own Adam group at 1e-3, init 0.05) was run warm-started from the K16r1 adapters (NDIAW: row-over-passage attention at the final side states; NKVW: side-layer states into K/V; NDIW: summary rows). All three tracked the warm controls WCa/WCb exactly and their `deltas off` panel equalled the full panel at every point. The `interventions` widget shows why: each layer's relative scale walked from 0.05 to ≈ 0 within 100 updates and stayed there. At init the untrained deltas cost 0.0008 nats, the gradient sign on the scale was therefore consistent, and Adam moves a parameter by ≈ LR per step regardless of gradient size, so 50 steps sufficed. The heads' gradients are proportional to the scale (unit rows × scale), so once the scale was gone the heads stopped learning — a dead path, not a null result on deep inputs. Relaunched with the relative scales frozen (`delta_scale_lr 0`) at 0.05 and at 0.2 of the calibrated RMS (NDIAWf / NDIAWF, NKVWf, NDIWf, NDILWf on ORCD; NDIAWnf, NKVWnf, NDIWnf, NDIAWnF on node 1 from the K16 init). Side finding: the residual RMS at the row positions (0.40 at layer 7) is 3× the calibration RMS measured on no-row sequences, so the relative scale should be read against the row-position RMS.

## 18. Store-side deep inputs are dead on arrival with a warm reader (21:00 UTC)

With the collapse-proof heads (zero-init output projection, no unit-RMS normalisation, no learned scale: `head_style lora`, tag `ac-recon-20260916T194707Z`), the 1/32 warm run A32DLn tracked the input-only A32X and its own deltas-off panel to ±0.001 at every point. The new `intervention_delta_rms` widget shows the mechanism: the applied delta rose to 0.5–1.6 % of the row-position residual within ~120 updates, then the optimiser drove it back down to 0.1–0.2 % by the end of the pass. Nothing collapsed this time; the heads simply learned that the reader does better without them. Together with the frozen-scale arms (finding 16) this closes the store-side family: residual or K/V deltas at the row positions, from the summary, from side-layer states or from row-over-passage attention, do not add usable bandwidth for a warm-started reader at 1/16 or 1/32. The reader's fetch (its own attention over 1/32 of the positions) is the bottleneck, so the next design replaces what the reader fetches: side-computed K/V in the row slots at every full-attention layer (`intervention_target kv_slots`, tag `ac-recon-20260916T205135Z`, arms A32KVS / NKVSW and twins), with reader-query cross-attention over the whole passage (`cross_attention`, HF-only) as the ceiling.

**Update 21:50 UTC — learned K/V slots do not help either.** A32KVSn (kv_slots, 1/32 warm, K16 init) finished at **0.3202** (acc 0.903) vs its deltas-off 0.3225 and the input-only A32X 0.3219: +0.002, inside the noise floor. The takeover scalars, free to move at LR 1e-3, stayed at −0.005…+0.005 for K and drifted to −0.03…−0.05 for V (the reader keeps its own row K/V and slightly amplifies V), and the slot content stayed within 5 % RMS of what it replaced. ORCD twins A32KVS / NKVSW (K16r1 init) tracked their deltas-off panels at 0.25. The reading: K/V written by a fresh head from side-model residual states is not in the format the reader's attention heads read, and a reader LoRA at 2e-5 over 1,024 updates cannot re-learn the query side, so the gradient keeps favouring the existing input channel. Round 6 (`kv_transfer`) therefore removes the learned head: the reader receives the side model's *own* pre-RoPE K/V at the row positions (same base model, so the format is native), with the takeover initialised at full replacement, an optional zero-init correction, optional placement at every full-attention layer, and `kv_transfer_full` (every passage position, HF-only) as the ceiling.

## 19. Native-format K/V transfer is the first deep design that beats the input-only control (23:15 UTC 09-16)

`intervention_target kv_transfer` (tag `ac-recon-20260916T220604Z`): at every full-attention reader layer the row positions' pre-RoPE keys and values are replaced by the side model's *own* keys and values at the same layer and positions — no learned head, takeover initialised at full replacement, trained in the `delta_scales` group. Because side and reader share the base model the replacement is in the reader's native format, so it is meaningful at step 0 (A32KVTAn: held-out 0.946 with the transfer vs 1.010 without, before any update) and the reader cannot "not read" it.

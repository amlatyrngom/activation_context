The activation-context model, end to end

A side language model reads messages and emits a short run of "view rows" that the target model reads in place of the text. This page shows the exact path a part takes through the model, how the rows are trained by self-distillation, and where the code and checkpoints live. It describes what is implemented in activation/ac_model/ (slice 3a), not a proposal.

  • Companion to SLICE3_PLAN.tressoir.md and SLICE3_ROUND.tressoir.md

What a part is

A part is a content element of a message. The target model's conversation can carry, next to ordinary text, an activation_context part that names the AC model, a compression ratio and the messages to compress. Parts nest: a part's messages may themselves hold parts (a compaction of a compaction, a subagent's compaction inside a parent's).

    {"type": "text", "text": "Task: ...\n\nTrajectory 1:\n"},

    {"type": "text", "text": "\n\nAnswer the question from the trajectories above. Reply with the answer only.\nQuestion: ..."}]}]

The number of rows a part becomes is fixed by its size under the side tokenizer: V = clamp(⌈Nside × ratio⌉, 4, 4096). A 32k-token span at ratio 1/16 is 2,048 rows; at 1/32, 1,024 rows. The target sees V positions where the text would have taken N.

The path of one part

Reading the diagram left to right: the part's own child parts are encoded first (recursively, through the same model) and adapted into the side model's input space; the part's messages are rendered by the side chat template with one placeholder run per child and tokenized; the side input embeddings get the child rows written over the runs and fixed sinusoidal positions; two bidirectional mixer layers spread information locally (blocked local attention: blocks of 256 rows attending to themselves and their two neighbours, never an N×N mask); windowed attention pooling shrinks the sequence to a quarter; V learned marker rows are appended and the whole sequence runs through the causal side decoder with its LoRA; the hidden states at the marker positions are the rows, sent through the target head (top-level part) or the recursive adapter (a child).

The head scales start at the RMS of the destination embedding table (0.019 for both Qwen3.5 sizes), so rows land at the magnitude the decoders expect from token embeddings.

How the rows are trained

Self-distillation on completions. The teacher is the target model reading the full text (with the same target adapter, no gradient); the student is the same target model reading the AC prefix, whose parts became rows. Both then read the same completion tokens; the loss is the full-vocabulary KL(teacher ∥ student) averaged over the completion positions. One optimizer, two groups: the AC modules and the side LoRA at 5e-4, the target LoRA at 2e-5.

Every completion is real text, so no sampling happens at item-generation time. The study questions come from the dataset study engine (dataset_study.py, qa on text chunks and traj_qa on trajectory chunks) and are cached under STUDY/.

A checkpoint is AC_MODELS/<name>/round_NNN/ (and a refreshed latest/) holding ac_modules.pt and the side LoRA folder; the target LoRA is checkpointed by the module manager under LORAS/<name>/round_NNN as before. Every save bumps the model version, which invalidates the row cache. ModuleManager.register_ac_model(config) builds the model, registers the side adapter when it is new, and loads checkpoint_path when given.

What the CPU checks showed

With Qwen3.5-0.8B as both side and target on this container (no GPU): a 1,095-token part becomes 64 rows in 5.8 s; a nested part encodes its child first (three network passes for a top-level text, a child and its parent); the second encode of the same part is a cache hit; a part encoded alone and inside a padded batch differ by at most 0.009 against a row RMS of 0.019 (bf16 decoder noise on CPU); gradients reach all 46 module tensors and the side LoRA B matrices; save, load and a version bump work. One trainer round on fabricated items brought the held KL from 3.19 (untrained) down within the first step; the node tests and the M9 runs give the real numbers.

The teacher forward is 46–69% of an example (34–45% of the ceiling, about 12k tokens/s on HF eager with the fla kernels), the backward 20–23%, the AC encode 7–11% (the side decoder is 85% of it), the head chunks 0.01 s. Two levers landed: the AC trainer now loads the fla kernel configs like the agent trainer (the test round went from 4,681 to 9,714 tokens/s), and teacher_cache_top_k keeps the teacher's top-k log-probs per completion position after an item's first pass so later epochs skip the teacher forward (the coarsened KL is within 0.1–0.4% of the exact one at k = 128; eval stays exact). The cap on one input is 72k teacher tokens (depth 2 at the default 32k threshold); 104k fits at 74 GB and was measured, not tuned for. About 30M in-context tokens per node hour with a live teacher, whatever the length mix above 8k.
